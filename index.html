Analyzing

    How it works

    Upload, analyze, edit

    Every suggestion comes with its reason, so you learn why a cut, color or sound fits your video.

    01

    Upload

    Drop your raw clip. CutCompass reads its length, size and shape to pick the right platform and structure.

    02

    Analyze

    Claude looks at 6 snapshots to name the video type and setting. Your browser measures pauses, light, colors and noise.

    03

    Get your plan

    Cuts, a color combination, sound levels for indoor or outdoor, and SFX ideas, each with the reason it fits.

    Your video stays on your computer

    Your browser decodes and measures the file itself. To recognize the video type, CutCompass sends Claude only 6 small still frames and the measurements, never the video or its sound. Close the tab and nothing is kept.

    FAQ

    Questions editors ask

    What does Analyze actually do?

    Your browser takes 10 snapshots and reads the whole sound track to measure pauses, speaking pace, background noise, voice level, brightness and colors. Then Claude looks at 6 of the snapshots with those numbers and writes the plan: the video type, cuts, a color combination, sound levels and SFX ideas.

    Why does it ask me to allow Claude?

    Recognizing the video type uses Claude on your own Claude account, so claude.ai asks once before the first analysis. If you say no, you still get a plan built from the measurements, and you pick the video type yourself.

    It got the video type wrong. What now?

    Open "Not right? Change it" under the result, pick the right type, location and mood, and press Update plan. The plan is rewritten for your choice.

    Which files work?

    MP4 (H.264 with AAC sound), WebM and most MOV files work in Chrome and Edge. Some iPhone HEVC clips don't decode in every browser. If that happens, export a quick H.264 MP4 and drop that instead.

    Does it edit the video for me?

    No. It plans the edit and lists every pause with its timecode. You make the cuts in Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro or CapCut.

    CutCompass combines measurements from your file with an editor's judgment. Treat the plan as a strong starting point and trust your eyes and ears in the final edit.
